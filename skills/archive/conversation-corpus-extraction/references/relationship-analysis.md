# Relationship Conversation Analysis

Use this reference after the corpus is complete and before writing a romantic or emotionally sensitive synthesis.

## Evidence matrix

| Dimension | Record directly | Do not infer automatically |
|---|---|---|
| Affection | explicit love, care, missing someone, bids for contact | commitment, compatibility, or a shared status |
| Contact intensity | counts, gaps, re-openings, initiator proxy, timing | who loves more or who is morally more invested |
| Status | each speaker's own words about together, exclusive, separated, friends, or undefined | a status from couple-like behavior alone |
| Future | named projects, dates, logistics, dependencies, follow-through | a plan from a wish or promise alone |
| Sex and body | comfort, pain, pressure, consent, pleasure, medical context | infidelity, an open-relationship request, or a performance verdict |
| Conflict | trigger, withdrawal, pursuit, wording, repair, recurrence | a diagnosis or a single-cause explanation |
| Practical support | requests, offers, constraints, delivered actions | entitlement or neglect without the surrounding agreement |

## Procedure

1. **State the boundary.** Give the chat, date range, timezone, record count, direction counts, and whether media or older history is missing.
2. **Extract status language.** Search for each speaker's explicit description of the relationship and preserve the date. If the descriptions differ, report the mismatch as the central finding rather than resolving it by semantics.
3. **Build a two-sided timeline.** For every rupture or repair, record the trigger, each person's immediate response, the next action, and whether the underlying agreement changed.
4. **Separate axes.** Summarize affection, safety, practical reliability, sexual comfort, exclusivity, and long-term commitment independently. Never collapse them into one investment score.
5. **Model the loop, not a villain.** Describe the observable sequence (for example, overload → withdrawal → pursuit → escalation → reassurance → no structural change) and mark the causal explanation as a hypothesis.
6. **Use historical context carefully.** Label prior-session or memory evidence with its date and source; it supplies context, not proof of the current state.
7. **Close with unknowns and next decisions.** List the smallest set of clarifications that would change the advice: status/exclusivity, concrete commitments, conflict-repair rules, sexual boundaries, and any medical or reproductive fact.

## Sensitive-media check

For an image or audio connected to pregnancy, health, paternity, money, or identity:

- Attribute it to the parent message's raw sender before interpreting it.
- Transcribe or describe only what is actually audible or visible.
- Compare all dates, measurements, labels, and metadata; flag contradictions.
- Do not authenticate the media or infer currentness, paternity, diagnosis, or legal/financial obligation from it alone.
- Ask for direct clarification before recommending an irreversible action.

## Output shape

Lead with a direct verdict, then give a dated timeline, the strongest evidence for each side, the interaction loop, uncertainties, and a short actionable next step. Keep facts, self-reports, and hypotheses visibly distinct; avoid diagnosing either speaker.
