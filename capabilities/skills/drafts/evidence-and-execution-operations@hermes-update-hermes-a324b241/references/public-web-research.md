# Public-web research depth

## Retrieval and escalation

Define the question, freshness, fields, sampling boundary, and permitted access before collection. Use search for discovery only, then prefer the approved extraction front door for known structured URLs and a real browser for clicks, forms, filters, login, JavaScript state, downloads, or visual inspection. Keep discovery, extraction, interaction, interpretation, and citation as separate evidence layers.

On a 403, 429, WAF, paywall, login wall, or bot interstitial, stop repeating the unchanged request. Try an authorized timestamped archive, same-host API/JSON/RSS/sitemap, configured reader, or real browser. Validate the body rather than status or byte count; reject redirects, login shells, rate-limit pages, and unrelated interstitials. Mark archived material as `snapshot` with its date and never present it as current price, availability, or news.

## Citation and synthesis

Register a source when retrieved and preserve its title, author, publication date, URL, retrieval time, query, locale, parser/version, raw artifact, and coverage boundary. Cite while drafting, use stable source IDs, attach exact quotes to high-stakes claims, render source lists mechanically, and verify every citation. Never treat a search snippet as support for a page body that was not retrieved; label genuinely unsourced claims `unverified`.

Reconcile sources explicitly and prefer primary records for dates, prices, legal status, metrics, and specifications. For conversation corpora, paginate fully and retain negatives, missing replies, edits/deletions, time gaps, thread boundaries, and sampling bias in the coverage record.

## Monitoring and public profiles

Freeze canonical entities, aliases, domains, event categories, cadence, cutoff, materiality threshold, source coverage, and the event deduplication key. Collect with overlap for late indexing; a failed source is a coverage gap and must not advance the cutoff or become “no news.” Collapse syndicated rewrites into one underlying event while retaining independent corroboration, then report event, date, source, change, significance, confidence, and follow-up.

Treat account discovery and identity confirmation as different tasks. Use public signals only, keep matches probabilistic, avoid sensitive-trait inference, and report conflicts rather than forcing a match.
